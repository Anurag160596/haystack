# Operation Uptime: questions Joveo's CEO is likely to ask, and the answers

Every number below comes from the delivered model (`Operation_Uptime_Model.xlsx`) or from a what-if run on a copy of it. "Show" names the cell or slide to put on screen. Sources are linked on the Assumptions tab, next to each input.

---

## A. The headline

**1. What exactly am I approving?**
€117.5k for 100 signed maintenance contracts by Fri 18 Dec. €99.8k is planned spend: €70.7k paid channels including €9k of tests, €27.4k for the three new sources, and €1.6k set-up. The other €17.6k is a reserve, released only by the Tracker rules from week 3.
- Excluded: Joveo's fees and the client's internal recruiter time.
- *Show:* Summary C5–C7, Budget C4–C12, slide 5.

**2. How sure are you of 100?**
100 if the funnel rates hold, with assessment slots 98% used (308 qualified for 314 slots).
- The risk is capacity, not money.
- Pass rates 10% worse → ~92 hires. 30% worse → ~71.
- The client can't do 55 a week and manages only 45 → ~83 hires.
- *Show:* Funnel B22–B26, slide 3 chart, slide 1 "How sure".

**3. Is €998 per hire cheap or expensive?**
Cheap.
- **German benchmark:** total cost of a skilled hire is €4,700, of which about a third (~€1,570) is recruiting (IZA DP 7656, 1,001 firms, https://docs.iza.org/dp7656.pdf, 2013 euros).
- **Agency:** 20–30% of a €37–45k salary (gehalt.de) = €7,400–13,400 per hire, so €0.74–1.34M for 100.
- **US comparison:** about $4,700 per hire (SHRM).

**4. Why not spend more to be safe?**
More money buys at most ~2 more hires: there are only 6 spare assessment slots, and qualified needed ÷ hires is about 3.
- After that the 55-a-week limit binds.
- The real safety lever is +9 assessment slots a week, which brings use down to 85%, the occupancy level where Bagust et al. (BMJ 1999, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10406748/) find risks start.
- *Show:* Budget B50, slide 1 asks.

**5. Why a 15% reserve?**
Three reasons:
- High-risk projects carry 10–20% contingency (Mastt).
- The reserve plus rounding (€17.6k) covers about 40% of the extra media a 30% miss on the qualified share would need (+43%, about €43k).
- IZA finds the cost of each extra hire rises when a firm hires 20 or more at once.

It is released only from week 3 by the Tracker rules. With no reserve the budget would be €100k.

**6. What do I get with 30% less (€82.25k)?**
About 87 hires.
- Electricians fall to ~21 of 30, supervisors to ~7 of 10.
- Mechatronics and automation stay almost full.
- Rule: cut the most expensive cost-per-hire cells first.
- Advice: keep the money; if forced, cover supervisors by internal promotion and protect electricians.
- *Show:* Budget rows 45–46, slide 6.

## B. Funnel and timeline

**7. Why 308 qualified applicants for 100 hires?**
Qualified → signed = pass × offer × acceptance.
- Electricians: 0.50 × 0.90 × 0.70 = 31.5%.
- Blended across roles: 32%, so 100 ÷ 0.32 ≈ 311 needed. The plan delivers 308 qualified, which means 100 hires because the cheaper new sources convert better.
- Sources: pass rates anchored on Gem 2025; acceptance on Königsteiner/StepStone (71–78%); 25% of applications qualified from IAB 2026.
- *Show:* Funnel rows 5–9, slide 2.

**8. Your 70–75% acceptance is far below SmartRecruiters' 91%. Too pessimistic?**
The 91–92% figure is a global benchmark. German surveys give 71–78%, so the plan uses those.
- If 91% held: budget €85k, slots only 77% used.
- That's upside, checked in week 4 once ≥7 offers are out (rule R10).

**9. Why is the pass rate the most sensitive input?**
Because slots are full.
- A lower pass rate means more qualified applicants per hire, and there are no spare slots to assess them, so money can't buy the hires back: 30% worse → ~71 hires.
- A lower qualified share or a higher cost per application only costs money (+43% / +30% media), with hires unchanged.
- Acceptance ties with the pass rate (same arithmetic). I name the pass rate because it depends on the plant's own test, so it is the least known and only readable after interviews; fast offers protect acceptance. Capacity ×0.7 also gives ~71, but 55/week is given in the brief, so it is shown for context, not ranked.
- *Show:* Funnel rows 41–50, slide 3.

**10. Why must applications arrive by 24 Nov?**
Work backwards from Fri 18 Dec:
- 7 days offer → signature
- 3 days interview → offer
- 8 days assessment → interview
- 6 days application → assessment

That gives 24 Nov. Supervisors add one extra round of 7 days, so their cut-off is 17 Nov.

18 Dec is the last date because job-search activity drops about 45% over the holidays (Indeed Hiring Lab DE).

**11. German time-to-hire is 55 days. You assume 24?**
Yes. It's a sprint, and it depends on client commitments: offers within 3 days, e-signature, and offers valid for 7 days. These are in the asks.
- Each extra day of offer approval costs ~8 assessment slots.
- If offers take 7 days instead of 3: ~92 hires (model run).
- The 6 + 8 days for screening and interview scheduling are SmartRecruiters' Germany medians.

**12. Is assessment the only bottleneck? What about interviewers?**
The brief gives assessment capacity only. In the model every qualified applicant who is assessed also takes the technical test or interview, so hiring managers need about 55 interview slots a week as well.
- Raise this as part of the "55 assessments/wk" ask.

## C. Channels and Joveo

**13. Programmatic is Joveo's core product. Why does it get only €8.7k and 4 hires?**
At the market-median Indeed cost of €161 per application (TalentBait, 2,549 DACH campaigns) and job-board quality, programmatic costs €2,317 per hire. That's more than StepStone (€1,564), LinkedIn (€1,687) or Meta (€1,963), so the model buys it last. Two points change the picture:
- **Joveo's platform runs LinkedIn, Meta and search too** ("Reach passive candidates across LinkedIn, Meta, search…", joveo.com), so most of the €70.7k of paid media can run through Joveo.
- **Joveo's own published manufacturing cases show cost per application down 17–32%.** Applied to programmatic, the budget falls to €112.5k–€107.5k and programmatic takes more hires (model runs).

These are vendor results, so they're shown as upside, not built into the base.
- *Show:* slide 4 footnote, Channels rows C05–C08.

**14. Why LinkedIn for blue-collar roles?**
Only for automation and supervisors (PLC/TIA and Meister profiles).
- LinkedIn reaches 15% of skilled-worker job seekers (Trendence 2023, 42,817 job seekers).
- Its cap = reachable in-market pool × 15% ÷ qualified share.

**15. Are Meta applicants any good?**
Lower quality: the social index is 0.50 of the average (IAB employer survey). The model already prices that in, at 12.5% qualified.
- Volume is capped at Redline's median of 20 qualified per Elektroniker campaign (181 trade campaigns).
- It still buys 6–7 electricians at ~€2,100 each.

**16. Why spend €9k testing every cell?**
That's 9% of the budget, matching the common "10% testing budget" practice.
- Every test reaches its €750 switch-off point by 9 Nov, so each channel is judged on real data.
- Test applicants are real candidates and their hires count. Duds are cut from Mon 2 Nov.

**17. How do you decide to move money each week?**
The Tracker rules, each sourced or statistical:
- **Scale up** if cost per qualified is 20% better than plan and slots are free.
- **Cut 50%** at ≥1.5×, **switch off** at ≥2× (ad-industry rules).
- **Switch off** a spent test with zero qualified, where at least 3 were expected (so bad luck doesn't kill a good channel).
- **Pause** the costliest cells if next week is forecast above 55 qualified; **release reserve** below 50.

The rules were tested: all 17 change a decision when actuals are typed in.
- *Show:* Tracker X column, slide 8.

**18. 20 free organic hires. Realistic?**
Organic volume = openings × 27 applications per vacancy at firms with 250+ staff (IAB) × 13% coming via the careers page (CareerPlug). Qualified share = 25% × careers-page index 0.71 = 17.8%.
- It is free media, but it uses slots like everything else.

## D. The new sources

**19. Is re-contacting past applicants legal?**
Only consented talent-pool records, typically kept for up to a year.
- Day-1 ask: a GDPR-checked export.
- The pool is 211 records: 10,703 staff × 7.8% in maintenance (BA occupation data) × 12% turnover (IAB) ≈ 100 hires a year, × runners-up per hire.
- At ~€14 a hire it's the cheapest source.
- If the export is empty, the budget rises to €130k.
- Joveo's AI Talent CRM does this re-engagement by email, SMS and WhatsApp, which makes it the repeatable product.

**20. Is a €1,000 referral bonus enough?**
Most German firms pay €501–1,000 (Personalwirtschaft/Radancy, 335 firms); hard-to-fill roles get €2,000 (KOFA example).
- Referrals are sized to deliver 11% of hires (CareerPlug); the model gets 11 hires.
- The bonus is paid only on a signed hire, so it comes out of the budget.

**21. Will construction electricians cut it?**
Only if the client accepts the profile. That decision is needed by 23 Oct.
- They face the automation-level pass rate (factor 0.9) and get a 2–4 week in-house bridge.
- 120 applications → 8.4 hires at €1,329.
- If the client says no, programmatic ads fill all 30 electricians and the budget rises to €127.5k.

**22. Polish corridor: legal, tax, language?**
- **Legal:** EU free movement, so no work permit.
- **Insurance and tax:** social insurance in the country of work (TK); German working days taxed in Germany, with no special commuter rule in the Germany–Poland treaty.
- **Recognition:** mechatronics is non-regulated, so none is needed.
- **Language** is the binding filter: 14% of vocationally trained Poles speak a foreign language well (Eurostat AES 2022). A Polish-speaking freelancer pre-screens applicants.

It delivers 18 hires (the 40% cap) at €383 each. Without it the budget would be €150k.

**23. Why not foreign-trained electricians?**
The brief says recognition takes 2–4 months, so the plan buys none. But in German law the industrial electrician is non-regulated for employees (BIBB: "This profession is not regulated in Germany"), so recognition is legally needed only for self-employment or the master-craftsman route.
- If the client accepts employer-verified competence for its Elektrofachkraft appointment, EU-trained electricians become an extra route before 31 Dec.
- That's an upside to raise.

**24. Why not just use an agency or temp workers?**
- **Agency:** €7,400–13,400 per hire, 6–11× this plan.
- **Temp:** billed at about 2× wage, capped at 18 months per worker, equal pay after 9 months. Taking a temp on permanently costs a fee of up to ~2 monthly salaries.

Temps work as a bridge for a few critical shifts, not as the plan.

## E. Risk and operations

**25. What is the single biggest risk?**
Assessment capacity: 98% used.
- **Mitigations:** pre-screened sources first (rediscovery, Polish pre-screen calls), weekly pacing ≤55, weekly "Full/OVER" flags, and an ask for +9 slots a week.
- With 64 slots a week, use falls to 84%.

**26. What if week-1 pass rates come in low?**
Once 10 interviews are in, if the pass rate is below plan (electricians 50%):
- tighten knock-out questions
- add pre-screen calls
- ask for more slots
- re-run the model

10% worse ≈ 92 hires.

**27. Why is electrician the hardest role?**
- **Scarcest trade in Brandenburg:** 85.7% of vacancies have no suitable unemployed candidate, vs 41.4% across all jobs (KOFA 2026).
- **Most money:** 38% of spend.
- **No alternative route** under the brief: no Polish route and no foreign-trained route.

Supervisors cost more per hire (€1,550) but are only 10 people. Automation has thinner channel coverage but only 15 hires.

**28. Doesn't promoting a technician to supervisor just create another opening?**
Yes. That's why internal promotion is only the fallback for a supervisor shortfall, not a source in the plan. Each promotion adds a technician backfill.

**29. How will you track and attribute?**
- **Data:** tracked URLs with utm tags captured in the applicant system's source field; a Monday export; recruiter disposition codes; the pre-screener sheet.
- **Tracker tab:** judges a channel once it has €500 spend or 30 applications (actual rates replace plan from 30 applications) and outputs scale/cut/off decisions plus a hire forecast.

**30. What does cash flow look like?**
- Weeks 1–2: about €34k (tests, StepStone ads, set-up).
- Weeks 3–6: €14–16k a week.
- Weeks 7–9: €2k a week of referral bonuses as people sign.
- Paid media stops 24 Nov.
- *Show:* Weekly M/N, slide 5 chart.

**31. What will we know by 9 Nov?**
- pass rate on the week-1 cohort
- each test cell's cost per application and qualified share
- size of the applicant-system export
- the client's construction-electrician decision
- first reads on the Polish corridor and construction-electrician tests (pass/kill bars on Initiatives rows 53–55)

## F. Can I trust the model?

**32. How do I know the spreadsheet is right?**
- An independent Python re-implementation recomputes 59 outputs.
- 152 scenarios pass, including edge cases.
- Each of the 183 inputs was changed one at a time with no errors and matching results; derived cells recalculate live from them.
- All 17 Tracker rules change a decision when actuals are typed in.
- The deck's 45 headline figures match the model.
- Report: `tests/README.md`.

**33. Which numbers are guesses?**
None are unexplained. Every input is one of three kinds:
- **A sourced fact** with a link: costs, rates, pools, reach, timings, volumes.
- **A live formula** on those facts, shown in black text: channel caps, rediscovery pool, referral volumes.
- **A stated rule or client commitment:** plain practice-based decision rules (cut at 1.5× plan cost, off at 2×, judge after €500 or 30 applications), the 3-day offer, the 18 Dec cut-off.

**34. Aren't vendor numbers biased?**
- TalentBait figures are medians across 2,549 campaigns.
- Joveo's case results are used only as upside, not in the base.
- Where vendor and neutral sources disagree, the model takes the neutral or more conservative one. For example, it uses the German 71–78% acceptance rather than SmartRecruiters' global 91%.

**35. If I change a number live, will the model follow?**
Yes. Every derived input is a live formula. For example:
- Change the qualified share, active-searcher share or reach → caps and costs move.
- Change openings → referral and rediscovery volumes move.
- Change the lags → the cut-off and StepStone ad counts move.

## G. Joveo's business

**36. Where are our fees?**
Not in the €117.5k. Joveo prices custom (media-based, volume discounts, a performance guarantee), so the fee is quoted separately.

**37. Is this repeatable for other clients?**
Yes, through three pieces:
- **Applicant-archive rediscovery** using Joveo's AI Talent CRM.
- **Tracked-to-hire attribution** using utm source tags.
- **A planning model driven by public statistics** (BA, IAB, Eurostat), so it adapts to any German region and trade by changing the inputs.
