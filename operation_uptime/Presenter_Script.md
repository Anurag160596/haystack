# Operation Uptime: presenter script (full deck, `Operation_Uptime_Deck.pptx`)

**Total time:** about 8–10 minutes, roughly 1 minute per slide plus 2 for slide 1.

**The one-line story:** "€122.5k gets 100 hires, but the real limit is 55 assessments a week, not money. So I pace everything around that limit, start with the cheapest sources, and watch the pass rate."

**Opening line:** "I'll give you the answer first, then show how I got there, where it can break, and how I'd run it."

---

## Slide 1: Summary (2 min)
**Headline to say:** "€122.5k gets 100 maintenance hires signed by 31 December, as long as you can assess 55 people a week."

**The three numbers:**
- **€122.5k:** €102.3k of planned spend plus a €20.2k reserve. The reserve is released only when a channel proves it works.
- **€1,023 per hire** (€1,225 if the reserve is spent).
- **98%:** the plan uses 98% of the 314 assessment slots. "Capacity, not money, is the limit."

**Risks (one line each):**
1. **Capacity:** "If assessments can't start on 22 October, we land at about 92."
2. **Electricians:** "I need your OK on construction electricians by 23 October. Without it, the plan costs €130k."
3. **Christmas:** "Offers within 3 days, e-signature, and spend stops on 24 November. A slower process costs about 8 hires."

**Asks:** 55 slots a week from 22 October, the past-applicant export, the €1,000 referral bonus, the construction-electrician decision, approval of €122.5k.

**Likely question: "Why a reserve?"** "Fifteen percent contingency. The rates are estimates, and I only release it when the weekly data says a channel is beating plan."

## Slide 2: Funnel (1 min)
**Headline:** "Working back from 100 hires, we need about 308 qualified applicants, and the client can assess 314."

**Logic:** go backwards through the funnel (electricians as the example).
- 50% pass the assessment, 90% of those get an offer and 70% sign: 0.5 × 0.9 × 0.7 = 31.5%.
- 30 hires ÷ 31.5% = **95 qualified**. About 1 in 4 applications is qualified, so about **381 applications**.
- All roles at standard rates: 311 qualified and 1,260 applications.
- Slots: 55 a week × 40 assessment days ÷ 7 = **314**.

**Say it:** "It fits, but with almost no headroom. That's why every week is paced to stay at or below 55."

**Likely question: "Why 308 and not 311?"** "Referrals and past applicants convert better than the standard rates, so the plan needs slightly fewer qualified people. It needs more applications, though (1,495), because some channels have a lower qualified rate."

**Likely question: "Why 24 November?"** "Work back from the last signature on 18 December: 7 days for the offer to be signed, 3 for the offer, 8 for the interview and 6 for the first assessment. Supervisors need applications a week earlier, by 17 November."

**Likely question: "Where do the rates come from?"** "IAB for the 1 in 4, Gem for pass rates, and German acceptance surveys. Each one is labelled Given / Researched / Own estimate in the Assumptions tab."

## Slide 3: Hardest role and sensitivity (1 min)
**Headline:** "Electricians are the hardest role. A 30% worse pass rate costs about 29 hires, and money can't buy them back."

**Why electricians:**
- **Scarcest trade:** 85.7% of Brandenburg electrician vacancies have no suitable jobseeker, against 41.4% for all jobs (KOFA).
- **Dearest last hire:** €2,272.
- **No back door:** no Polish route, and foreign-trained electricians need 2–4 months of recognition.

**The chart logic:** I made each input 30% worse, one at a time.
- **Pass rate ×0.7:** we'd need 440 qualified people for 314 slots, so only about 71 hires.
- **Share qualified or ad cost 30% worse:** still 100 hires, it just costs more (about €185k at worst).

**Say it:** "Problems that cost money are fixable. Problems that use up slots are not. That's why I read the pass rate on 2 November."

**Likely question: "Acceptance also shows 71. Why the pass rate?"** "Fast offers protect acceptance. The pass rate depends on the plant's own test, and we only learn it after the interviews."

## Slide 4: Channels (1 min)
**Headline:** "Every role buys its hires from the cheapest source first."

**Logic:**
- Cost per hire = cost per application ÷ share qualified ÷ share of qualified who sign.
- Rank the sources and fill each role from the cheapest, until each source runs out:
  - Organic: free
  - Past applicants: €14
  - Polish corridor: €383
  - Referrals: €1,000
  - Construction electricians: €1,329
  - StepStone: €1,564
  - LinkedIn: €1,687
  - Programmatic: €2,142, which fills the remaining gap
- Meta and Google Search are dearer (€2,388 and €3,442), so they get a €750 test only.

**Say it:** "About 42 of the 100 hires come from the cheap new sources. Paid channels only fill what's left."

**Likely question: "Why is programmatic the biggest spend if it's expensive?"** "It's the only channel big enough to cover the electrician gap once the cheaper sources run out."

## Slide 5: Budget and weeks (1 min)
**Headline:** "€102.3k planned across roles and weeks, plus a €20.2k reserve."

**Logic:** money follows the assessment slots, because an empty slot is lost forever.
- **Week 1 is biggest (about €32.5k):** its applications must fill 11 days of slots, 22 October to 1 November.
- **Tests:** €9k in weeks 1–2, 12 tests of €750 each.
- **Moving money:** weak channels are cut from 2 November and tests are judged by 9 November. The reserve is released from week 3, only on results.
- **Paid media stops on 24 November.** Weeks 7–9 are only referral bonuses, paid as people sign.

**Likely question: "Why not spread it evenly?"** "Because slots are the limit. Front-loading means week 1 fills its slots, and the tests tell me where the later money goes."

## Slide 6: Smaller budget (1 min)
**Headline:** "With 30% less (€85.75k), we sign about 87, and electricians fall short first."

**Logic:** keep the 15% reserve and cut the most expensive cost-per-hire channels first. Those are mostly electrician and supervisor channels.

**Result:**
- Electricians: 20.5 of 30
- Supervisors: 8.1 of 10
- Mechatronics: 44 of 45
- Automation: 14.5 of 15

**Say it (to the client):** "The last €36.75k buys about 13 hires, mostly electricians, the people who keep the lines running. Keep it. If you must cut, agree up front that supervisors are covered by internal promotion."

## Slide 7: New sources (1.5 min)
**Headline:** "Three new sources supply about 42 hires, and one is built for electricians."

**Each source uses the same five points: who, how, maths, test, who does what.**
- **I1, past applicants plus referrals:**
  - About 211 runners-up get email and SMS waves; referrals pay a €1,000 bonus.
  - €63 plus €11k in bonuses gives about 42 qualified and about 16 hires.
  - Pass bar: ≥14 qualified. Kill bar: <8.
- **I2, construction electricians:**
  - About 10,000 within 75 km, which more than doubles the hardest pool.
  - 118 applications give 30 qualified and 8.4 hires, at €1,329 each.
  - Needs your profile sign-off and a 2–4 week bridge course.
- **I3, Polish border corridor:**
  - About 1,700 Polish workers already commute to Brandenburg (IAB).
  - A Polish-speaking freelancer pre-screens language before anyone uses a slot.
  - 18 mechatronics hires at €383 each, exactly at the 40% cap.

**Joveo product line:** "Past-applicant rediscovery is repeatable. Every employer has consented past applicants nobody markets to, and Joveo's tracked links plus Talent CRM make it a source in any market."

**Likely question: "What if the export is empty?"** "Then the plan costs about €135k. Programmatic fills the gap."

## Slide 8: Run it (1 min)
**Headline:** "Track by source and role every week, move money on rules, test the riskiest assumptions early."

**Rules:**
- Judge a channel only after €500 or 30 applications.
- 20% cheaper than plan: add 25%. 50% dearer: halve it. Twice the plan cost: switch it off.
- Over 55 qualified next week: pause the dearest channel. Under 50: release reserve.

**Checks by 2 November:**
- Pass rate
- Construction electricians' qualified share
- Real test costs

**Week 1:**
- Tuesday 20 October: kick-off
- Launch channels and tests
- Wednesday 21 October: past-applicant wave
- Friday 23 October: construction campaign goes live
- Monday 26 October: first read-out

**Closing line:** "So: €122.5k, 100 hires, and the one thing I need most from you is 55 assessments a week from 22 October. Happy to take questions."

## Tough questions to have ready
- **"How confident are you?"** "100 if the funnel rates hold, with no slack. 10% worse pass rate gives about 92; 30% less budget gives about 87. I'll know which world we're in by 2 November."
- **"Why not hire more Polish workers?"** "The brief caps Polish hires at 40% of mechatronics, which is 18. I'm already at the cap, and every Polish hire is tracked by residence."
- **"Why not foreign electricians?"** "Recognition takes 2–4 months, so they can't sign and start by 31 December."
- **"How do you run this from Bangalore?"** "Everything is digital: tracked links, ads, and a Monday ATS export. The local pieces are a Polish freelancer and the client's own trainer and assessors."
- **"What did you check yourself?"** Answer this one honestly, in your own words.
